Read together: a week of my activity notes

The earnings-call post built a graph linking text worth reading together. Here is the same experiment on a different kind of text: the short summaries an AI assistant writes of my computer activity, every ten minutes and every six hours. The pile is 422 summary files from Sep 23–30, 2026 (399 ten-minute summaries and 23 six-hour summaries). Each file is one snippet: its title, description and summary text (the app list and the file-path citations are left out).

  • JEV: the pile is small, so instead of sampling we asked JEV about every one of the 88,831 pairs, for $2.04. Same question as the post.
  • Embedding similarity: Qwen3-Embedding-4B, cosine.
  • TF-IDF: scikit-learn, English stop words removed, cosine.

1. Which method finds real insights?

Each method ranked every pair, except the 335 pairs where a six-hour summary sits next to one of its own ten-minute parts (trivially related). GPT-5.6-Luna then scored each method's top 100 from 1 to 5, with the post's question reworded for activity notes: does reading the two together give a specific, non-obvious insight, such as a link between separate projects, a shared cause, a contradiction or a forgotten follow-up?

2. How JEV's answers look here

JEV says “yes” to 97% of all pairs; the middle 80% of weights sit between 0.54 and 0.79 (median 0.67). That is expected: every note is from the same person's week, so almost any two can be read together usefully. So the ranking matters more than the raw numbers. Over all pairs, JEV's ranking correlates with the similarity methods (Spearman rank correlation: TF-IDF 0.71, Embedding similarity 0.68), but their very tops differ: JEV's top 100 shares 2 pairs with Embedding similarity's; JEV's top 100 shares 0 pairs with TF-IDF's.
