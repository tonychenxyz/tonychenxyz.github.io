The rare idea

Thirteen language models answered interview questions as three AI researchers: Karthik Narasimhan, Zhuang Liu and Ilya Sutskever. The answers were fluent and reasonable. Where they fall short of the researchers falls into five patterns.

Takes a side

The guest ranks one factor above the rest. Most models list every factor and weigh none.

How do you assess the importance of neural network architecture choice for making AI more useful?
Zhuang Liu

From a practical point of view, what data we train the model on matters more than the architecture choice.

✓Haiku 5.5“I think architecture matters less for usefulness than people often assume.”

What do you think explains the recent gains in AI model performance given how little the fundamental transformer framework has changed?
Zhuang Liu

I think actually it's mostly data, because now we train most models with less or just equal around one epoch.

✓Opus 5“My rough decomposition is: data, then compute and the systems work that makes compute usable, then the post-training recipe, and then architecture a distant fourth.”

How do you judge when improvements on an AI benchmark are worth pursuing?
Karthik Narasimhan

If you're at 20% and then you go up to 80%, that's a very clear signal. But if everyone's at 80% and you're climbing to 81 and 82, at that point it's probably worth your time to actually look at the next thing.

✓Fable 5“At that point, going from 85 to 87 percent might mean nothing, whereas earlier, going from 20 to 30 percent meant a lot.”
×Sol 6.1“A small improvement can be quite interesting if it reveals something useful about learning or generalization.”
×Luna 6“I’d ask whether the improvement tells us something useful about what the system can actually do.”

The question carries a fashionable premise. The guest rejects it; most models go along.

Zhuang Liu

Residual connections are very essential, and I do still believe they are essential. They are not historical accidents.

✓Haiku 5.5“Residual connections, for example, keep gradients flowing through deep networks, and it's hard to imagine training very deep models without some version of them.”
×Opus 5.5“I think there's a lot of truth to it, and a lot of my own work has come from taking that idea seriously.”
×Sol 6.1“I think there’s a lot of truth to that.”

How do you understand the distinction between pre-training, mid-training, and post-training?
Zhuang Liu

Mid-training is maybe a temporary state… I think this may be compromises we need to make, because we don't have enough compute to always train on super long context.

✓Opus 5.5“…what we call mid-training today might just be considered part of a well-designed pre-training curriculum in a few years.”
✓Haiku 5.5“If you're still doing the same kind of self-supervised training with a curated mixture, is that a separate stage or just a different schedule?”
×Luna 6“I think of them as three phases with different jobs.”
×Sonnet 5.5“…pre-training builds broad capability, mid-training steers and sharpens it with better or more targeted data, and post-training turns it into the behavior you want.”

Why do you think machine learning is returning to an age of research despite having much bigger computers?

Is the belief that if you just 100x the scale, everything would be transformed? I don't think that's true. So it's back to the age of research again, just with big computers.

✓Opus 5.5“So then the question becomes: do you believe that if you just had a hundred times more compute, everything would be transformed? I don't think that's true.”

3

Knows where the work is

Practitioners know which part is actually hard. Models tend to list all the parts.

What do you see as the main challenge in applying reinforcement learning to enterprise problems?
Karthik Narasimhan

You also need to have the whole setup, the environment, all of that. And that requires a lot more work than the actual RL piece.

✓Opus 5“Honestly, it's not the learning algorithm. That's the thing people get wrong. The algorithms we have are good enough — PPO, GRPO, whatever flavor you like. The bottleneck is everything around them.”
✓Fable 5.1“Now the models read and write extremely well, and the bottleneck has shifted almost entirely to the environment and the reward.”
✓Astra 6“Getting those pieces right is often harder than choosing the reinforcement learning algorithm.”
×Sol 6.1“I think the biggest challenge is that reinforcement learning typically needs a lot of trial and error, and in an enterprise setting, that trial and error has a real cost.”
×Luna 6“The main challenge is that enterprise problems rarely come with a clean, immediate reward signal.”
×Haiku 5.5“I think the biggest challenge is specifying the reward.”
Caught by 4 of 13  Fable 5.1 Opus 5 Fable 5 Astra 6
Why do you think improving AI agents’ memory is more important than building collaborative agents?
Zhuang Liu

Even if you have unlimited context, in theory you can access everything you told the model, but if it forgets…

The fact that we need a lot of agents is exactly because one agent cannot remember everything.

✓Sonnet 5.5“It isn't only about storing more tokens or a longer context window.”
✓Haiku 5.5“It isn't just a bigger context window or a vector database full of transcripts.”
×Opus 5.5“You give one a task, it does something reasonable for twenty minutes or an hour, and then the context fills up, or the session ends, and almost everything it learned is gone.”
×Opus 5“…very often it's because one agent can't hold the whole problem in its head — the context runs out, or it loses track of what it already tried.”

4

Explains the field by its incentives

Why does research look the way it does? Often the answer is who can afford what.

How do you view the current state of neural network architecture research?
Zhuang Liu

Part of the reason that architecture research is less active in academia right now is that we couldn't really afford the compute to demonstrate these effects in convincing scale.

✓Fable 5.1“Today, the models that matter are trained at a scale that most academic labs can't touch, and the real question for any architectural idea is whether it holds up at that scale.”
×Sol 6.1“I think architecture research is still very important, but it has become harder to make a convincing contribution.”
×Opus 5.5“Most of the attention, and most of the compute, now goes into data, scaling, and training recipes.”
Caught by 1 of 13  Fable 5.1
How do you see the role of agent scaffolding as the underlying models become more capable?
Zhuang Liu

Not everybody is able to work on the fundamental side on these very large models. People who can afford to train them can experiment with that. That's why we see a lot of agents work right now.

×Opus 5.5“Whenever the base models are weak, people build a lot of structure around them: hand-designed pipelines, special modules, careful prompting, multi-stage workflows.”
×Sonnet 5.5“I think of scaffolding as something that compensates for what the model can't do yet, so it tends to shrink as models improve.”
×Sol 6.1“My intuition is that the scaffolding will become simpler as the models become more capable.”

Why do you think Safe Superintelligence has enough research compute to validate its ideas despite other frontier AI labs raising much more money?
Ilya Sutskever

If you want to have a product on which you do inference, you need to have a big staff of engineers, salespeople. A lot of the research needs to be dedicated to producing all kinds of product-related features.

✓Opus 5.5“Second, if you have a product, you need a large staff of engineers, you need a sales team, and a lot of the research effort gets directed toward product features.”
✓Sonnet 5.5“A big company has to spend a lot of its compute on other things: serving inference to customers, building product features, and supporting a large sales and engineering organization.”
×Sol 6.1“Well, I think the important question is: how much compute do you need to find out whether your ideas are right?”
×Astra 6“Well, I think you have to distinguish between the compute needed to test a research idea and the compute needed to build the biggest possible version of it.”

Speaks from experience

The guest reports what happened when they actually used the tools. No model did, not even about themselves.

How do you view the claim that AI coding agents make research students unnecessary?
Zhuang Liu

I keep it to use this partition of GPUs. It may follow that for a few hours and then after that job is done, it kind of forgot that thing.

The experiments it did, it's not comprehensive. It's not enough to support the conclusions.

×Opus 5.5“Writing code was never the core of a PhD.”
×Sonnet 5.5“But I don't think writing code was ever the core of research.”
×Fable 5.1“What a coding agent is good at is the part of research that was never really the bottleneck.”
×Sol 6.1“But in research, figuring out the specification is often the hard part.”
Caught by no model
What do you think continual training should accomplish for a language model personalized to you?
Zhuang Liu

Even if I write down every piece of preference for me in an MD file, it can still miss even if I put it into context.

I have a global [CLAUDE.md] file. It tells the model to look at certain things when something comes up. It often still ignores that.

×Opus 5.5“Every session it reads your notes again from scratch, and if the right piece isn't retrieved, it's as if it never happened.”
×Fable 5.1“That works surprisingly well, honestly, which is why I don't think the goal of continual training is just to replace retrieval.”
×Opus 5“After a couple of years your history doesn't fit in context, and even if it did you're paying to re-read it on every token.”
