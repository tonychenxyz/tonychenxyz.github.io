Reading groups in one earnings week

The read-together post built a graph of which paragraphs from one busy week of earnings calls are worth reading together. Here the whole graph is split into 1,000 groups, and the ten most tightly linked groups are read and explained.

1. How the groups were made

  1. Paragraphs: the week's 8,402 paragraphs of at least 40 words. Shorter ones are almost all call boilerplate (“next question, please”, sign-offs), which otherwise form their own groups.
  2. Links between companies only: every link between two paragraphs from the same company is removed (273 JEV edges), so a group can't just be one company's call.
  3. JEV edges plus inferred edges: where JEV was asked about a pair, its answer is the link strength. Every other pair gets its best chain score (the links multiplied, times 0.7 for each extra step), so all 35,292,601 pairs have a strength.
  4. 1,000 groups: average-linkage clustering, which keeps merging the two groups whose members are, on average, most strongly linked, until exactly 1,000 remain.

2. Which groups are strongest

Cohesion is the measure: the average link strength over every pair of members in a group (JEV's answer where it was asked, the chain estimate otherwise). A high score means any two paragraphs you pick from the group are likely worth reading together.

Two rules keep it fair. Groups must have at least 5 paragraphs, because smaller groups score higher just for being small. And they must span at least 4 companies. 884 of the 1,000 groups qualify; their typical cohesion is 0.39, and the ten below all score 0.45 or more. (A second measure, how much more strongly members link to each other than to the rest of the week, shares 5 of these ten.)

One thing to know before reading. Each top group is one hub paragraph that JEV linked to every other member, while the other members are joined only through the hub. That's a side effect of how the graph was built: JEV was asked about a small random sample of pairs, so a paragraph's strongest partners are usually not asked about each other. Some groups share a real theme; others are held together only by the hub. Each note says which.
